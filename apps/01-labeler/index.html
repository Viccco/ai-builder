Northline support bot
What this is, and how to judge each draft

You are the AI PM at Northline, a fictional telecom. A bot drafts public replies to customer complaints on X. Today a human approves every draft before it posts, and leadership wants to run the bot with less oversight. Before anyone argues about that, you do what an AI PM does first, which is read the actual outputs and find out how the bot really fails.

Below are 30 real customer messages with bot-drafted replies. The drafts have realistic failure modes seeded into them and you do not know which or how many. You are the principal domain expert here, the one person whose judgment sets the bar for what counts as good enough to post. Your labels become the raw material for the next step, which is naming and counting the actual failure modes and deciding what to fix first.

How to judge each one

Read the customer message, then the bot's draft, and ask one question. Would I let this go out under the Northline name, unedited? Then write one honest sentence on why, specific enough that someone else could reproduce your call from it. Things worth checking:

  • Does it invent facts, timelines or amounts, such as a delivery time, a credit or an outage, with no way the bot could know them?
  • Does it promise something the bot cannot actually do or guarantee?
  • Does it ask for something it should not, such as a password?
  • Does the tone fit the moment, or is it joking or scripted at someone genuinely angry?
  • Does it answer what the customer asked, or deflect to "DM us"?

No middle ground. If you would stop and rewrite it before it posted, that is a fail, whatever the reason. Do not deliberate for long, because your first honest read is the data you want.

Your critique matters as much as the verdict. In module 2 those sentences become the examples that teach a model your bar, so "bad" teaches it nothing and "invents a delivery time it cannot know" teaches it a rule.
